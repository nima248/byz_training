Daily Immersion

Immersion is the traditional way of teaching chanting. Apprentices stand at the analogion for many years, deeply absorbing the sound world of Byzantine music.

These audio files are designed as an approachable way to build a habit of daily chanting practice. They are short, structured and practical. Consistent short sessions are much better than a long session once or twice a week. If we want to absorb and retain music, we need to constantly remind our brains that it is important. That means: Daily Immersion!

Your phone speaker is fine, but an external speaker will allow you to hear better. Any cheap bluetooth speaker is adequate. If you want to use headphones, open over-ear headphones are better. Earbuds and headphones that enclose the ear can interfere with how we hear our own voice.

The basic routine is a 2 minute warmup and one audio exercise. Done in less than 10 minutes!

How to warm up

Get in the habit of always warming up your voice properly before chanting. The voice is fragile, and if injured it can be very difficult to restore. Even without significant other practice, doing a regular vocal warm up will build your vocal strength and stamina. Even if you can't manage any other practice, there is always an opportunity to warm up. Once you know how you can do it anywhere.

Suggested 2 minute warm up:

  • Massage up and down the back of your neck.
  • Roll your shoulders forwards and backwards.
  • Slowly pivot your head side to side, then up and down, extending and tucking your chin.
  • Two deep breaths in through the nose, then out slowly through the mouth with tight lips, as if blowing through a straw.
  • Take a big yawn and relax your body.
  • Sing with lip trills (buzzing the lips), or "vvv". Slide up and down like a siren. Gently increase the pitch range and volume. At least 1 minute.

How to do exercises

During exercises, follow the instructions.

  • Listen: Just listen! No humming.
  • Audiate: Explained below.
  • Sing: Sing by yourself.
  • Together: Sing along and match what you hear.

Audiation is hearing music in your mind without any external sound. No chanting or humming allowed - only hearing mentally. You probably won't literally hallucinate sounds, but you will gradually develop a mental awareness, a knowing of what the sound is. You probably audiate when you read - you can hear, in some sense, what the words sound like, and imagine that someone is saying them.

At first audiation will feel difficult, or like you aren't really doing anything. This is normal, and you are not doing it wrong - you are slowly re-wiring your brain. Just relax and trust the process. All you have to do is try, and your brain will do the rest. Resist the temptation to sing or hum, because then you wouldn't be audiating anymore.
